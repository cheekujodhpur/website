On the occasion of Gandhi Jayanti, please join me in enjoying what I believe are *mahaan* things about the *Mahatma*.

It wasn't until quite recently, November 2025, that I visited the Gandhi Ashram in Sabarmati. Until then, I did not have any special appreciation for him beyond regarding him as another freedom fighter. Well, I know that he's the Father of the Nation and all that, but that all happened long before I was born, and there's not much I feel about those titles. I remember that I also had a phase in school when I discovered all the controversial things about MK Gandhi and I would peacock my contrarian secret knowledge to my classmates.

In Ahmedabad, my experience was coloured by visiting several religious places of consequence — Hindu, Jain and Muslim. There was a lot of messaging about peace and brotherhood, and places of worship devoted to relatively lesser known saints of Jain and Hindu sects have a lot of explanation about the moral vessel their originator saint was; a goody-good member of society, pious and selfless and so on. In contrast, Gandhi Ashram has better real estate, but one of the galleries is called "My life is my message" and a large wall with this...message.

![Sabarmati Ashram](/images/sabarmati1.jpg)
*Image via [Savaari Blog](https://www.savaari.com/blog/gandhi-sabarmati-ashram/)*

This is a trivial statement, but it really got me thinking about his life once again, in contrast with the world and society as I know it when I am 30 years old living in India. That is a good segue for you to enjoy this [banger from Lage Raho Munna Bhai](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8tDfkeUsX7c)

He decided to live his beliefs. He fought a fight which he had little personal incentive to fight. His spiritual beliefs, while controversial from several perspectives, inspire the notion of introspection to know what is justice. Do we really need the heavens to inform us how we shall treat other humans? We already know the answers. Do we need them written in a book to guide how we shall conduct our lives?

Ironically, Gandhi ji also had a banger of a favourite bhajan which is now practically devoted to him. Let us go with [Manoj Kumar in Purab Paschim's version](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nBKqlE4qlr0).

Look, no matter what anyone tells you, Ramayana is about a moral vessel who lives a life that inspires others. It is exactly like Harry Potter or Lord of the Rings in that sense. The "lead character" does not have any consequential ideas of their own but they decide to stand firmly behind what they believe is right. And hence, this ego of viewing himself as a moral vessel or in devotion of Lord Ram fits well.

The second lesson after introspection is compassion. Compassion is a non-measurable emotion. You must cultivate an abundance of it, also towards your enemy. With that spirit, Gandhi would go on to lead non-violent movements against the British. He had firm belief that they would see the error in their ways if they were forced to introspect.

Again, it is irrelevant if this theory holds. Gandhi believed in this and hence he lived by this. His life was his message, and what a powerful one at that!
